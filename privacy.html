VoxGuard Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 21, 2026 · Applies to the VoxGuard app and this site.

VoxGuard is a consumer safety tool that analyzes voice scam risk during protection sessions you start yourself. This policy describes what the app actually does today — not aspirational behavior.

Microphone

  • The microphone is requested only after you explicitly start a Live Mic protection session. VoxGuard never asks for microphone access at launch, and never listens in the background.
  • Raw Live Mic audio is processed in memory only. VoxGuard does not store a raw microphone recording.
  • If you are entitled to transcript-backed analysis and cloud transcription is configured, Live Mic audio is streamed to the configured transcription provider (AssemblyAI) for the duration of the session only to produce transcript text. If transcription is unavailable or not in your plan, analysis continues on-device with acoustic anomaly indicators only.

Analyze Recording

  • You choose a file and then choose a privacy mode. Nothing uploads on file selection alone.
  • Keep on this device: decoding and analysis run locally; zero network calls. You may optionally paste a transcript you already have — it is analyzed locally.
  • Include conversation analysis: the selected audio is uploaded once to the VoxGuard transcription relay, which forwards it to the configured speech-to-text provider. VoxGuard does not permanently store the uploaded file; the original file on your device is never copied into VoxGuard storage.

Incident reports (on-device)

  • Flagged sessions save a local incident report on your device: an ID, timestamp, a SHA-256 digest of the analyzed audio, risk score, threat reasons, transcript snippets, and source labels. Reports persist locally (capped list) until you delete them.
  • Incident reports never contain audio and never leave the device unless you share one yourself.

Family Shield

  • Trusted Circle names and phone numbers you enter are stored locally on your device (maximum 5 contacts) and are never sent to VoxGuard servers.
  • A Family Shield alert is sent only when you tap to send it after a flagged session. The alert payload contains privacy-minimal metadata: an opaque VoxGuard identity (vg_…), an incident reference, a risk band, an analysis-scope flag, and later a Safe / Still Suspicious response — never audio, transcripts, names, or phone numbers.
  • Alerts travel through a VoxGuard-operated relay which holds the OneSignal credentials; OneSignal delivers the push notification to devices that opted in. An "accepted" result means the relay accepted the request — it is not a guarantee of delivery to a specific device.
  • Notification permission is requested only from the Family Shield receiver card in Settings — never at launch.

Preferences & personalization

  • During first-run setup you may optionally enter a display name (for example, "Maya"). It is stored locally on your device only (SharedPreferences), trimmed, capped at 32 characters, and shown on your own Home screen. It is never included in Family Shield payloads, never synced, and never tied to an account — there is no account.
  • Your interface language (English, العربية, Español, Français), theme (System / Light / Dark), accent color, text size, experience mode (Standard / Guided), motion, and haptics preferences are all stored locally on your device and never transmitted.
  • Interface localization is presentation only: conversation-risk analysis currently supports English and Egyptian Arabic regardless of the interface language.

Purchases

  • Subscriptions are handled by RevenueCat and your app store (Google Play / App Store). VoxGuard receives an entitlement state (which plan is active) — we do not see or store your payment details.
  • Web and desktop builds use a clearly labelled Demo Store that simulates checkout and never charges money.

What we do not do

  • No accounts, no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK, no cross-device tracking.
  • No contact-book or call-log access. VoxGuard cannot intercept native cellular calls.
  • Direct Groq semantic analysis exists as a developer-only experiment and is disabled in production builds; the production semantic engine runs on-device.
  • Threat Score is an assistive risk signal — not a probability, not a forensic or legal determination.

Data retention & deletion

Incident reports and Trusted Circle contacts live in local app storage on your device.

  • You can delete an individual incident report inside the app (open the incident, then use the delete action). Deletion is permanent on this device.
  • You can remove a Trusted Circle contact from the Trusted Circle card at any time.
  • Uninstalling the app removes all locally stored app data.
